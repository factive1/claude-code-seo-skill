# Claims ledger

The durable artifact of an audit, and the thing that makes the second run cheaper than
the first. Copy this into the project being audited, fill it as verification proceeds,
and load it into every writing and fixing agent afterward.

Without it, each agent re-derives the same facts and gets them wrong in new ways.

## Capabilities that do NOT exist

The highest-value section. Every entry here was found asserted in live content.

**Sales-adjacent sentences are where invented capabilities appear.** In one audit, ten
were found and nearly all sat in a CTA or closing pitch — the moment a writer needs a
reason to act, they reach for a plausible-sounding feature. Grep every CTA before the
first rewrite, not after the ninth is found.

Record what a study *measured*, not just its number. Most bad citations come from
stretching a real study past its scope — a finding about restaurants quoted as a finding
about contractors, an attitudes survey quoted as observed behavior.

## Contested facts — claim deleted

When two competent verifications of a checkable fact disagree, delete the claim rather
than pick a side. In one audit, five independent attempts to count the categories on a
single policy page produced five different totals. The number added nothing for the
reader and would have broken the next time the page was reorganized, so the article now
describes the structure and links out.
