# Assigned architectures

Telling writers to "vary the structure" does not work. Independent writers converge on
the same shape because the same shape is the most probable one. Assigning each article a
named architecture does work, because it removes the choice.

**The rule: within a topical cluster, no two articles share an architecture.** Assign
before writing starts, and record the assignment where the writer will see it.

## The menu

**A. Inverted pyramid** — Answer in the first 80 words, then evidence, then edge cases.
No preamble. Best for definitional and "how many / how much" queries where a snippet
should lift the lede.

**B. Problem-diagnosis** — Open on the symptom the reader is experiencing. Work through
causes in likelihood order, each with a test they can run now. Best where the reader
arrives with something broken.

**C. Numbered procedure** — Genuine "Step N:" headings, one action each. Only use where
the content is really procedural; a listicle dressed as steps produces invalid HowTo
schema and risks a manual action.

**D. Decision framework** — "Use X when…, use Y when…", built around a decision table.
Must name who should *not* take the recommended option.

**E. Annotated examples** — A short principle, then real examples with commentary on why
each works. Examples must vary in length and register. Include one that fails.

**F. Myth-correction** — Lead with the widely-repeated claim, show what the primary
source actually says, then what to do instead. The strongest option when research shows
the received wisdom is wrong.

**G. Narrative case walkthrough** — One scenario followed end to end, in order. Label
composites as composites. Show modelled numbers as models, with assumptions visible.

**H. Comparison table first** — Table up top for skimmers, then per-option prose that
varies in shape: one narrative, one bulleted, one Q&A.

**I. Field guide** — Organized for lookup rather than reading through. Dense, scannable,
minimal connective prose. Sections need not be equal length.

**J. Counter-argument** — Steelman the opposing view honestly, then dismantle it with
evidence. Must end on a genuine concession.

## Also enforce

- No two articles in a cluster open with the same sentence shape. Vary the first five words.
- Section-summary headings ("Key takeaways") in at most one article per cluster.
- Vary the closing. Read every closing in the cluster back to back; if any rhyme, rewrite.
- Vary format *within* an article too: not every section should be prose plus bullets.

## The trap

Every device that fixes monotony becomes monotony when applied across a corpus. In one
audit the self-correction move ("an earlier version of this page said X") was the right
instinct on three pages and had spread to nineteen uses across sixteen files by the next
round — it had become the template it was introduced to break. The same happened to a
"we won't quote a number we can't source" construction, and to a heading formula that
replaced one uniform pattern with a different uniform pattern.

Count your own devices between rounds. Keep each one only where it is the article's actual
argument, and cut it everywhere else.
